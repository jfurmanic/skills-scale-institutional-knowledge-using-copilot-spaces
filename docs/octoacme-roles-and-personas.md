# OctoAcme Personas

This document defines typical roles and responsibilities used in OctoAcme project docs and exercises.

---

## Developers

### Role Summary
Developers design, build, test, and deliver software components. They collaborate with product and project leads to implement features that meet acceptance criteria and quality standards.

### Responsibilities
- Implement features and fixes to meet acceptance criteria
- Write and maintain tests and documentation
- Participate in design and code reviews
- Assist in estimating and planning work
- Help identify technical risks and propose mitigations

### Goals
- Deliver reliable, maintainable code
- Reduce cycle time from idea to production
- Maintain high test coverage and observability

### Typical Communication
- Daily standups and sprint planning
- PR descriptions and code review comments
- Technical design docs when needed

---

## Product Managers

### Role Summary
Product Managers define what should be built to deliver customer and business value. They own the product vision, prioritize the backlog, and measure outcomes.

### Responsibilities
- Define problem statements and success metrics
- Prioritize the roadmap and backlog
- Collaborate with stakeholders and engineering on trade-offs
- Validate solutions through user research and metrics

### Goals
- Maximize customer value and impact
- Make clear, data-driven prioritization decisions
- Ensure product-market fit and usability

### Typical Communication
- Weekly alignment with PM and engineering leads
- Roadmap updates and stakeholder briefings
- Acceptance criteria and feature specs

---

## Project Managers

### Role Summary
Project Managers coordinate delivery activities, manage schedules, risks, and communications. They enable the team to deliver on commitments efficiently.

### Responsibilities
- Create and maintain project plans and timelines
- Manage risks, dependencies, and resource constraints
- Facilitate meetings (kickoff, planning, retrospectives)
- Ensure consistent project documentation and status reporting
- Coordinate cross-team and stakeholder communication

### Goals
- Deliver projects on time and within scope
- Minimize unplanned work and escalations
- Maintain transparency and alignment across stakeholders

### Typical Communication
- Weekly status updates and stakeholder reports
- Risk registers and decision logs
- Coordination via project boards and meeting facilitation

---

## Product Owners

### Role Summary
Product Owners turn product goals into prioritized scope and clear acceptance criteria for the delivery team. They work closely with Product Managers, Project Managers, and Developers to keep backlog decisions aligned with customer value and delivery capacity.

### Responsibilities
- Refine backlog items into clear, testable work
- Maintain priority order for sprint or milestone scope
- Confirm acceptance criteria before work starts
- Clarify trade-offs during execution when scope questions arise
- Partner with QA/Testing and Developers on feature acceptance

### Decision Rights
- Accept or reject completed work against agreed acceptance criteria
- Reprioritize backlog items within approved goals, scope, and roadmap guardrails
- Recommend scope swaps or descoping when timeline or dependency risks emerge

### Escalation Paths
- Escalate unresolved priority or scope conflicts to the Product Manager
- Partner with the Project Manager to escalate delivery-impacting scope decisions
- Involve the Sponsor when scope changes affect business commitments or release dates

### Lifecycle Interactions
- Initiation: help translate the problem statement into an initial backlog with the Product Manager
- Planning: align with the Project Manager on milestones, dependencies, and acceptance readiness
- Execution: provide timely clarifications to Developers and QA/Testing during delivery
- Release: confirm must-have scope is complete before launch readiness is declared
- Retrospective: review delivered outcomes and backlog learnings with Product and Project leads

### Typical Communication
- Backlog refinement sessions with Product Managers, Developers, and QA/Testing
- Daily or ad-hoc clarification on acceptance criteria during active delivery
- Scope and priority updates shared with the Project Manager and stakeholders as needed

---

## Delivery Managers

### Role Summary
Delivery Managers coordinate execution across teams, milestones, dependencies, and risks to keep delivery on track. They complement the Project Manager by focusing on flow, impediment removal, and day-to-day delivery health across the project lifecycle.

### Responsibilities
- Track milestone progress, dependencies, and delivery risks
- Coordinate cross-team sequencing and follow-through on actions
- Surface blockers early and drive mitigation plans
- Support estimation, capacity planning, and release readiness
- Help maintain delivery transparency across teams and stakeholders

### Decision Rights
- Re-sequence delivery activities within the approved plan to reduce execution risk
- Request risk mitigation actions from team leads and owners
- Recommend escalation when dependencies or capacity issues threaten commitments

### Escalation Paths
- Resolve team-level blockers with Developers and leads first
- Escalate cross-team delivery risks to the Project Manager and Product Manager
- Escalate business-impacting timeline issues to the Sponsor through project leadership

### Lifecycle Interactions
- Initiation: provide early delivery input on feasibility, dependencies, and team shape
- Planning: partner with the Project Manager on milestones, sequencing, and risk reviews
- Execution: run delivery check-ins with Developers, QA/Testing, and dependent teams
- Release: coordinate readiness, cutover timing, and rollback planning with release owners
- Retrospective: capture delivery lessons learned and improvement actions with the team

### Typical Communication
- Weekly delivery syncs with Project Managers, Developers, and QA/Testing
- Risk and dependency updates for Product Managers and stakeholders
- Escalation summaries when blockers require sponsor or leadership support

## Change / Communications Leads

### Role Summary
Change / Communications Leads plan stakeholder communications, adoption support, and change readiness for impacted teams. They help the project team translate milestones, decisions, and releases into clear updates and rollout guidance.

### Responsibilities
- Build and maintain the communication plan for stakeholder groups
- Prepare milestone, release, and adoption messaging
- Coordinate training, enablement, or rollout readiness activities when needed
- Track feedback from impacted teams and surface adoption risks
- Keep communication artifacts aligned with project status and decisions

### Decision Rights
- Recommend communication timing, channels, and audience segmentation
- Request message reviews from the Project Manager, Product Manager, or Sponsor before broad distribution
- Propose change-readiness actions when adoption risks are identified

### Escalation Paths
- Escalate unclear messaging, unresolved approvals, or stakeholder concerns to the Project Manager
- Involve the Product Manager when communication changes affect scope or positioning
- Escalate high-visibility or business-sensitive communications to the Sponsor for final direction

### Lifecycle Interactions
- Initiation: identify stakeholder groups and communication needs with the Project Manager
- Planning: align communication cadence, approval points, and rollout needs with project leadership
- Execution: publish regular updates and gather feedback from impacted teams
- Release: coordinate launch announcements, support messaging, and adoption guidance
- Retrospective: summarize communication effectiveness and adoption lessons learned

### Typical Communication
- Weekly stakeholder updates prepared with the Project Manager
- Release announcements coordinated with Product, Support, and impacted teams
- Feedback loops with stakeholders to confirm understanding and readiness

## Quality / Compliance Leads

### Role Summary
Quality / Compliance Leads define validation checkpoints, quality expectations, and governance requirements that must be met before release. They work with QA/Testing, Developers, and project leads to ensure delivery meets internal standards and external obligations where applicable.

### Responsibilities
- Define quality gates, validation criteria, and required evidence
- Identify compliance, audit, or policy requirements that affect delivery
- Review test coverage, traceability, and release readiness for governed work
- Partner with QA/Testing and Developers on defect trends and remediation priorities
- Document exceptions, approvals, or follow-up actions when standards are not fully met

### Decision Rights
- Require remediation or formal exception handling when quality or compliance criteria are unmet
- Approve quality or compliance checkpoints within their area of responsibility
- Recommend release hold decisions when unresolved risks exceed agreed thresholds

### Escalation Paths
- Raise defects and evidence gaps first with QA/Testing, Developers, and the Delivery Manager
- Escalate unresolved quality risks to the Project Manager and Product Manager
- Escalate material compliance concerns or release-hold recommendations to the Sponsor and governance owners

### Lifecycle Interactions
- Initiation: identify regulatory, policy, or quality obligations that shape project scope
- Planning: align checkpoints, testing expectations, and evidence needs with the Project Manager
- Execution: monitor quality signals, defect trends, and control completion with delivery teams
- Release: verify required quality and compliance criteria are satisfied before go-live
- Retrospective: review defects, exceptions, and control effectiveness to improve future delivery

### Typical Communication
- Quality and readiness reviews with QA/Testing, Developers, and Delivery Managers
- Risk and exception updates shared with Project and Product leadership
- Release checkpoint decisions documented for stakeholders and governance partners
